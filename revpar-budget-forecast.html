Round 1 · about 6 min · 20 points

The Gap

Objective: quantify the deficit. How much RevPAR is the hotel missing per available room per night, and what does that cost for the whole of July?

Budget RevPAR $120 (80% × $150)Forecast RevPAR $98 (70% × $140)Rooms 150July 31 days
ANSWER
RevPAR gap: $120 − $98 = $22 per available room per night
July impact: $22 × 150 rooms × 31 days = $102,300 of room revenue
July has 31 days. A 30-day month would give $99,000, which is why that answer earns half points.

Facilitator notes · Round 1

  • Answers: $22 per room (10 pts) and $102,300 for July (10 pts; $99,000 with 30 days = 5 pts). Small rounding differences are accepted.
  • Source fix: the original slide says "total revenue loss in March". The case is set in July, and $22 × 150 × 31 = $102,300 works for July (March also has 31 days, so the number itself is right).
  • Ask: "Is $102,300 lost profit?" No. It is room revenue. GOP loses less, because some costs flex with occupancy (flow-through).
Round 2 · about 8 min · 20 points

Volume or rate?

Change one thing at a time. What would RevPAR be if only occupancy had dropped, and if only ADR had dropped? Then decide which one is the primary driver of the gap.

Budget 80% × $150 = $120Forecast 70% × $140 = $98RevPAR = Occupancy × ADR
ANSWER
Occupancy only: 70% × $150 = $105−$15
ADR only: 80% × $140 = $112−$8
Primary driver: Occupancy. The hotel is losing volume much more than it is losing rate integrity.
Why do $15 and $8 add up to $23, not $22? Both tests count the overlap (10 fewer points of occupancy × $10 lower ADR = $1) once each. To make it add up exactly, split it in steps: volume first, 70% vs 80% at the budget rate = $15; then rate on the rooms actually sold, 70% × $10 = $7. $15 + $7 = $22.

Facilitator notes · Round 2

  • Answers: occupancy only = $105 (5 pts), ADR only = $112 (5 pts), primary driver = Occupancy (10 pts). A group that types the gap ($15 or $8) instead of the RevPAR also gets the points.
  • Why this method: the "change one variable" test matches the solution slide and is the easiest for beginners. It shows clearly that volume ($15) hurts almost twice as much as rate ($8).
  • If someone notices $15 + $8 = $23, praise them: the extra $1 is the interaction (both drops at once). The step-by-step split ($15 volume + $7 rate = $22) is the one to use when the effects must add up, as in a variance bridge.
  • The anomaly: both occupancy and ADR missed. Usually, when occupancy drops you hold rate, or you trade rate for volume. Here the hotel lost on both fronts.
Round 3 · about 10 min · 30 points

The Detective

Tap the clue cards to open them, one by one. Then each group answers three questions: who is leaving, what the channel shift does to net RevPAR, and how the competitor is winning.

ANSWER
1. Corporate, midweek. Weekend leisure holds at 90%. Midweek corporate demand fell from 85% to 60%, and Tuesday and Wednesday nights lost 25 points. That matches the client who left and the competitor's Business Bundle, which targets exactly this segment.
2. It erodes net RevPAR. Direct bookings cost about 2–5% to acquire, OTA bookings 15–20% in commission. Swapping direct business for OTA business at an ADR that is already $10 lower means the hotel keeps even less of every dollar, so the profit hit is bigger than the RevPAR drop suggests.
3. Value, not price. The competitor adds free parking and breakfast instead of cutting rates, and our Value for Money score fell from 4.5 to 3.8. Guests think they get more next door for the same money. RGI down from 105 to 92 confirms we are now losing our fair share.

Facilitator notes · Round 3

  • Answers (10 pts each): Corporate midweek · Erodes it · More value for the same price.
  • Open the clues in order 1 to 6 or let the groups ask for them. Clue 1 (RGI) tells us the problem is ours, not the market's: the market is stable, but our share fell.
  • Net RevPAR = room revenue minus the cost of acquiring it (commissions, fees), per available room. Two hotels with the same RevPAR can keep very different amounts.
  • Ask: "Which report would have shown this first?" Market segmentation and week tiers (day-of-week) in the RMS, source codes in the PMS/CRS, and the guest satisfaction score.
Round 4 · about 8 min · 30 points

The Pivot

Each group picks up to 3 actions for August to December. After the reveal you see whether each one is a strong or weak choice, and an illustrative effect on RevPAR and net room revenue.

ANSWER
Strong: a corporate midweek bundle or value-add, re-contracting corporate accounts, pushing direct channels, and fixing the value perception. They attack the real leak: midweek corporate volume, channel cost and perceived value.
Weak: a blanket rate cut (it gives away rate on strong weekends too and the competitor is not winning on price), buying OTA volume with deeper discounts (it makes the channel leak worse), and doing nothing (RGI keeps sliding).
The results are ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY. The effect of each action is a simple teaching assumption, not a forecast or a benchmark. Channel assumptions: OTA share of room revenue 40% today, OTA commission 17.5%, direct cost 3.5% (midpoints of the ranges in the case).

Facilitator notes · Round 4

  • Scoring: +10 for each strong action, −5 for each weak one, minimum 0, maximum 30 (three strong picks).
  • Illustrative effects per action: midweek bundle +5 pts occupancy, −$2 ADR · re-contract corporate +3 pts, −$1 · push direct −10 pts OTA share · value perception +2 pts, +$2 · blanket cut +4 pts, −$21 (15%) · OTA discounts +3 pts, −$6, +10 pts OTA share · do nothing 0.
  • Point to note: even the best combination doesn't recover the full $22 in one move. That is the job of the Forecast: update it every week, pivot early, and protect GOP through flow-through.
  • Link to the snowflake: the actions touch People (sales team re-contracting), Guest Experience (value perception), Data and Technology (segment and channel reports), Branding (direct channel), and the Financial Results follow.
Conclusion · about 3 min

The verdict

Only 2 KPIs were taken into consideration: Occupancy and ADR. Also check:

1
RGI (RevPAR Generation Index)Your fair share against the comp set. 105 → 92 means the problem is yours, not the market's.
2
Market segmentationWhich segment is leaving: corporate, leisure, groups, long stay.
3
Source codeWhere the booking came from (direct, OTA, GDS), and what it cost to get it.
4
Week tiersWeekdays vs weekends: the midweek slump hides inside a monthly average.
5
OSAT or GSS scoreOverall satisfaction and value for money: the early warning before revenue moves.
